Okay, I would like to work on language and scripts for TerraMythica. Tongues and dialect.

Here’s a framework for the tongues and scripts of TerraMythica—laying out the major language families, their regional dialects, and the writing systems that developed alongside them.

1. Language Families & Major Tongues
Celivaric Family (High-magic heartlands)

High Celivar (“The Court Tongue”): Used in royal courts, formal magic rites, and diplomacy.

Low Celivar (“Common Celivar”): Spoken by townsfolk; more relaxed grammar and a simplified honorific system.

Sylvan Celivar: A poetic offshoot used by forest-dwelling mystics; features extra nasal vowels and lilting intonation.

Tarian Family (Mountainous & nomadic peoples)

Ironpeak Tarian: Harsh, guttural consonants—reflecting the clamor of forge-halls.

Windstep Tarian: Spoken by caravaneers; drop final consonants for speed of speech.

Snowbound Tarian: Softens many stops to fricatives, suited to cold winds.

Ar’Khaic Family (Ancient coastal city-states)

Old Ar’Khaic: Preserved in temple inscriptions; largely extinct as a spoken tongue.

Neo-Ar’Khaic: Modernized version used in trade guilds—borrows heavily from Common Celivar.

Mariner’s Ar’Khaic: Dialect with a specialized nautical vocabulary and unique “sea-sibilants.”

Drun’Gothic Family (Deep caverns, subterranean realms)

Stone Drun: Throaty, uses click-like stops to echo in caverns.

Torchlight Drun: More melodic, developed for singing epic ballads at torch-lit feasts.

2. Writing Systems
2.1 Celivaric Script (“Arcanis Script”)
Type: Alphabetic with magical diacritics

Characteristics:

Twenty-two base letters; each can take one of three “rune-dots” to indicate magical tone.

Written left-to-right, flowing cursive form for spellbooks; formal charters employ a blockier “Stonehand” variant.

Example Glyphs (block form):

𐌁 (b), 𐌂 (c), 𐌃 (d)…

2.2 Tarian Runes
Type: Featural syllabary

Characteristics:

Consonant strokes share basic shape families; vowel modifiers add hooks or bars.

Carved into wood or bone—so glyphs are angular.

Direction: Top‐to‐bottom columns, right‐to‐left within each column.

2.3 Ar’Khaic Sealmarks
Type: Logographic + phonetic phonograms

Characteristics:

Roughly 300 “root” logograms for common concepts (water, sea, trade, oath);

A set of ~50 phonograms to spell foreign names or technical terms.

Material: Painted on plaster or incised in marble.

2.4 Drun’Gothic “Echo Script”
Type: Tactile glyph‐lines (intended to be read by touch)

Characteristics:

Deeply incised lines and pits; use of raised dots to distinguish homographs.

Ideal for low‐light environments where sight is limited.

5. Notes on Script Usage & Literacy
Magic & Celivaric Script: Only formally schooled scribes and mages command full “Rune-Dot” orthography; commoners learn a pared-down “Basic Arcana” alphabet.

Tarian Runes: Passed down orally and through apprenticeship—no standard school; each clan’s runes vary slightly.

Ar’Khaic Sealmarks: Mastery requires years in temple scriptoriums; seals still bind pacts and trade contracts.

Drun Tactile Script: Taught from childhood in deep-kingdom academies; partially inaccessible to surface-dwellers.

This system gives you a scaffold to play with phonetics, cultural flavor, and the look‑and‑feel of each script. You can now start drafting sample alphabets, constructing word lists, or even composing short dialogues in any of these tongues!


Let's work on the Written Traditions an also Who records history.

Below is an overview of how TerraMythica’s peoples preserve knowledge on the page—and who is charged with keeping those records safe through the ages.

1. Forms of Written Tradition
Royal Annals

Format: Year-by‑year chronicles, often on long parchment scrolls or bound vellum codices.

Content: Succession of rulers, major battles, treaties, dynastic births/deaths, natural omens.

Style: Very formal; heavily footnoted with “Rune‑Dot” annotations when magic influenced an event.

Temple Chronicles

Format: Large stone tablets or marble stelae in temple courtyards, sometimes supplemented by illustrated manuscript “Booklets of Revelation.”

Content: Theological debates, liturgical developments, miracles witnessed, pilgrim registries.

Style: Mixture of Old Ar’Khaic logograms for sacred concepts and Neo‑Ar’Khaic phonograms for narrative.

Bardic Song‑Scrolls

Format: Thin strips of treated animal hide, rolled and fastened with leather ties.

Content: Heroic epics, genealogies of great houses, ballads of legendary Emblematic deeds.

Style: Written in Torchlight Drun script or Sylvan Celivar cursive, often with musical notation in the margins.

Guild Manuals & Trade Ledgers

Format: Bound tamarisks‑wood covers with Ar’Khaic Sealmarks on the front, sheets inside.

Content: Technical diagrams, production methods (e.g., forging techniques), merchant contracts, shipping logs.

Style: Neo‑Ar’Khaic script for text; small inset Celivaric marginalia for magical safeguard clauses.

Secret Codices & Grimoires

Format: Locked iron‑clasp tomes, sometimes written on thin sheets of crystallized plant sap (“glass‑vellum”).

Content: Forbidden rites, lost Emblem origins, “dark” treatises on feeding the emotion‑beings.

Style: Arcanis Script with intentional script‑errors as magical locks; only readable by those knowing the “dot‑key.”

3. Who Records History
Royal Scriptorium

Location: Each capital city’s “Hall of Sundials.”

Personnel:

Chronicle Masters: Lead historians who oversee accuracy and political framing.

Runecallers: Mages who inscribe “Rune‑Dots” to imbue the text with wards against alteration.

Scrivener Apprentices: Young scribes learning the block‑style “Stonehand” Celivaric.

Temple Scriptorium

Location: Inner cloisters of major temples (e.g., the Prism Sanctum of Ar’Kha).

Personnel:

Archivist‑Priests: Combine theological training with paleography to interpret Old Ar’Khaic.

Illuminators: Artists who add iconography—often painted in living root‑pigments.

Custodial Acolytes: Ensure the great tablets remain clean of moss and lichen.

Bardic Circles

Location: Traveling caravans, forest hermitages, subterranean feasting halls.

Personnel:

Lore‑Singer: The lead bard, responsible for composing and memorizing epic traditions.

Scroll‑Binder: Crafts the physical scroll from hide; marks ownership with clan‑specific seals.

Memory‑Keepers: Junior bards who memorize and recite texts when scrolls are too fragile.

Guild Scriveners

Location: Merchant ports, ironforges of the Ironpeak Clan, urban trade halls.

Personnel:

Master Scrivener: Edits and authorizes all official guild manuals and ledgers.

Ledger‑Keepers: Update stock counts, shipping manifests, and contract renewals daily.

Seal‑Engravers: Carve official Ar’Khaic Sealmarks into tablets and binding covers.

Hidden Scribes of the Umbral Covenant

Location: Secret libraries beneath ruined fortresses or in isolated caves.

Personnel:

Shadow‑Chroniclers: Record the “dark histories”—the fall of magical beings, forbidden experiments.

Glyph‑Wardens: Encode texts with anti‑scrying wards so only Covenant members can read them.

Archive‑Phantoms: Invisible familiars that whisper marginal notes as new secrets emerge.

4. Interplay & Conflict
Political Control: Kings sometimes “borrow” Temple Chronicles to legitimize rule, provoking friction with Archivist‑Priests.

Bardic Freedom: Bards regard themselves as “keepers of truth,” and often clash with Royal Scriptoriums over artistic license.

Guild Secrecy: Trade secrets are guarded fiercely; leak a guild manual and face both legal and magical reprisal.

Forbidden Knowledge: The Umbral Covenant’s codices are hunted by both mages seeking power and clerics fearing corruption.
